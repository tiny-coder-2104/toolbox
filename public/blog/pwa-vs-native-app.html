PWA vs Native App: Which Should Freelance Devs Build for Clients?

Clients ask for "an app." What they usually mean is: "I want something on people's phones." The question is — do they need a native app, or will a Progressive Web App do the job?

For most freelance projects, the answer is PWA. Here's why.

When to Build a Native App

  • Hardware access — Bluetooth, NFC, ARKit, advanced camera features
  • Background processing — continuous GPS tracking, audio playback in background
  • App Store presence — client specifically wants to be in the App Store / Play Store
  • Complex animations — games, 3D rendering, heavy GPU usage

When a PWA is Enough

  • Content sites — blogs, portfolios, documentation
  • E-commerce — product catalogs, checkout flows
  • Tools — calculators, formatters, converters
  • Dashboards — admin panels, analytics views
  • Business apps — CRM, invoicing, inventory

The Freelancer's Playbook

Here's how to pitch PWAs to clients:

1. Start with the problem, not the technology.
Don't say "let's build a PWA." Say: "Your customers want to access your catalog offline. I can build that in 2 weeks for $3,000."

2. Show the math.
A native app typically costs $20k+ and takes 3 months. A PWA often costs $3k and takes 2 weeks. Both solve the same problem for most use cases. (Typical ranges — your numbers will vary.)

3. Demo the install.
Open a PWA on your phone, tap "Add to Home Screen," and show the client the icon. That's the moment they get it.

4. Mention the upgrade path.
"We can start with a PWA. If you need native features later, we can wrap it in Capacitor or build a native shell."

Real Example: Client Billing App

A client wants an app where contractors log hours and generate invoices. Do they need native?

  • No hardware access needed
  • No background processing
  • Works offline (service worker caches the app)
  • Needs to be installable (home screen icon)

PWA is perfect. Build it in a week, deploy to Vercel. No Play Store fees, no approval process. Pricing depends on your market and client.
